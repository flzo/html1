Climate change means that the Earth's weather is becoming warmer and less stable. Scientists say that one of the main causes of climate change is human activity. Over the past 150 years, people have burned a lot of coal, oil, and gas for energy. These fuels give us electricity, heat, and power for cars, factories, and airplanes. However, when they burn, they release gases such as carbon dioxide into the air. These gases trap heat around the Earth, like a blanket, and make the planet warmer.

Another cause of climate change is cutting down forests. Trees absorb carbon dioxide and help clean the air. When people cut down too many trees for wood or to make space for farms, the Earth loses an important way to control carbon dioxide. Sometimes forests are burned, which makes the problem worse by adding more gases to the atmosphere.  

Farming and raising animals also cause climate change. Cows and other farm animals release a gas called methane, which is very strong at trapping heat. Some farming methods use chemicals that also add harmful gases to the air.

Factories and industries add even more greenhouse gases. Making cement, steel, and other products requires a lot of energy, which often comes from burning fossil fuels.

Even daily human activities, like driving cars or using a lot of electricity, add to climate change. Each small action may not seem like much, but together, they have a big effect on the planet.

In short, climate change is caused mainly by burning fossil fuels, cutting down forests, farming practices, and industrial activities. Understanding these causes is important, because it helps us find ways to reduce the problem and protect our planet for the future.
