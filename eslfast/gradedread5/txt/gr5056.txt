Gene and cell therapies are new types of medical treatments that use the body's own cells or genetic material to fight diseases. Instead of just treating symptoms, these therapies try to fix the real cause of the problem inside the body.
Gene therapy works by changing or adding genes in a person's cells. Genes are like instructions that tell our bodies how to work. Sometimes, a person is sick because a gene is missing or not working correctly. In gene therapy, doctors may add a healthy gene or repair the broken one. For example, gene therapy is being studied for blood diseases, certain cancers, and even some types of blindness.
Cell therapy is a little different. In this treatment, doctors use living cells to heal the patient. Sometimes, the cells come from the patient's own body, and sometimes they come from a donor. These cells can be changed or grown in a lab before being put into the patient. A well-known type of cell therapy is stem cell therapy, which can help replace damaged cells in the body. Another example is CAR-T cell therapy, where special immune cells are changed to better fight cancer.
Both gene and cell therapies give hope to people with diseases that are hard to treat with normal medicine. However, these therapies are still very new. They can be expensive, and scientists are still studying how safe and effective they are.
In the future, gene and cell therapies may help cure diseases that we cannot cure today. They show how science and medicine are moving forward to give people better and healthier lives.
